LPs tilt to smaller managers as plans to cut rosters jump to 15%; BC Partners eyes PIF's LIV loan and Bahrain courts family offices

Top stories
LPs

LPs now prefer managers under $500m, and 15% plan to cut their rosters

SS&C Intralinks' 2027 LP Survey (370 LPs and allocators, answers collected in Q3, published 5 Nov) found 49% prefer allocating to managers with under $500m, up from 28% a year ago. The share planning to add managers fell to 65% from 75%, while the share planning to cut rose to 15% from 4%. Nearly one-third say they won't allocate without a credible AI strategy. Smaller managers have a pitch; everyone else should expect harder re-up conversations.

Virginia Retirement System terminated a $591m AQR Market Neutral Value mandate and hired ValueAct for $200m

VRS's 4 Nov Investment Advisory Committee materials recap moves since mid-August: the AQR termination took effect 31 August, ValueAct Strategic Global Fund II (a co-investment vehicle) was hired for $200m, and Irenic Vantage got $100m plus $25m across two co-investment vehicles. Board books like this are where mandate changes surface before press does.

BC Partners wants to buy PIF's $50m LIV Golf bankruptcy loan

The FT reported on Sunday that BC Partners is preparing a preliminary bid for the debtor-in-possession loan, which carries control of LIV's Chapter 11 case, days after its LIV 2.0 plan collapsed. PIF put more than $5bn into the league. Reuters could not verify the report. It is a live case study of how a Gulf sovereign exits a strategy that no longer fits.

Gulf

Bahrain plans a trust-law overhaul and a multi-family office framework

The Economic Development Board's CEO told AGBI on Monday that the draft would remove the time limit on trusts, make sharia compliance optional and clarify when a family office needs a licence. No start date was given. The UAE hosts 53% of MENA family offices versus Bahrain's 5% (HSBC, 2024), so this is a challenger play, but worth tracking for family-office coverage.
