Bridgewater co-CIO Karen Karniol-Tambour leaves to run Canada's new sovereign wealth fund

Prime Minister Carney named her first CEO of the Canada Strong Fund on Monday: C$25bn of initial capital over three years, arm's length with its own board, co-investing in federally backed projects and selling principal-protected Canada Strong Notes to retail. Bridgewater CEO Nir Bar Dea confirmed the exit to Reuters; she transitions out over the next few weeks. Expect Bridgewater clients to ask about CIO succession on their next call, and a new sovereign allocator to put on coverage lists.

LPs

APG, manager for Europe's biggest pension fund, ends active equity investing

APG told Bloomberg the internal active equity strategy (about €33bn) is being stopped, cutting 40 jobs including the 22-person stock-selection team, after its active book fell 1.6% in 2025 and lagged over five years. Public equities go to customized index tracking for ABP; APG stays active in fixed income, real estate, infrastructure and private equity. Another big-ticket vote that active fees must be earned in privates and alternatives, not in liquid equities.

NL Times citing FD; Al Khaleej citing Bloomberg →
Perf

HFRI fell an estimated 1.4% in October, with about 60% of funds down

Equity hedge lost 2.4% (fundamental growth -5.6%, technology -4.8%) and macro 1%, while activists gained 3.6%, sovereign fixed income 5.3% and the Relative Value index 0.2%. CTAs were roughly flat at -0.1%. This is the number allocators will quote back at you in Q4 reviews, and the dispersion gives strategies outside equity a clean story to tell.

Jain Global's equities head Townie Wells exits to launch his own fund, in talks with Millennium

Bloomberg reports Wells left last week to start a long/short equity fund and is discussing a potential multibillion-dollar allocation from Millennium plus outside capital. Jain Global returned all external money in September and now runs only for Millennium. Goldman research cited in the piece says about half of 2025 launches were seeded through SMAs, up from 16% a decade ago. Single-source (Bloomberg, via Hedgeweek).
