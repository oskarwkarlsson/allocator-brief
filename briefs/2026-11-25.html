Gulf

A Houthi drone hits Riyadh’s PIF Tower again, injuring 11

Saudi Civil Defense said 11 people (two Saudis, two Indians, three Nepalis and four Egyptians) were hurt on Tuesday when a drone struck a tower in the King Abdullah Financial District; witnesses and the Civil Defense photo point to the PIF Tower, the sovereign fund’s headquarters and Riyadh’s tallest building. It is the second strike on the tower in six days, after material damage on 19 Nov. The coalition promised a “firm response”, and the UAE condemned the attack. For anyone with PIF, Hassana or KAFD meetings on the calendar, expect travel-risk reviews and more requests to move meetings to video or to Abu Dhabi during ADFW (7 to 10 Dec).

55% of hedge funds expect to need more prime financing; a quarter of those trust their brokers to supply it

The first quarterly StoneX Prime Insight Report (survey of 57 senior executives by Acuiti, Sep to Oct) found 42% of all firms expect financing balances to rise over 12 months versus 7% expecting a fall, and 55% among hedge funds, yet only a quarter of those expecting more are confident their current primes will deliver. Service and responsiveness (37%) is the top complaint, ahead of cost (26%), and 26% plan to add prime brokers. In fixed income, 43% expect conditions to worsen, with cross-margining hardest to get. Useful for ODD meetings: allocators will ask how many primes you have and how stable your financing is in stress.

Launch

Kairos launches a daily-liquid UCITS fund that hires hedge funds through SMAs

Anima-owned Kairos Partners in Milan goes live this month with €50m of seed, starting with long/short equity, trend following and systematic macro, with an arbitrage manager (convertible, credit, merger) next. It targets established managers, with tickets of €40m to €50m, one new manager per roughly €50m of growth and a cap of seven or eight, and it can put up to 10% in commingled funds. Aimed at private banks, pensions and multi-asset buyers. A concrete new ticket for UCITS-ready managers who can run a dedicated account, and further proof that daily liquidity sells in Europe.

Rules

Hong Kong drafts tax breaks tied to each pod’s results, not the fund’s

Draft guidance seen by Bloomberg would let performance-linked bonuses at multi-manager firms qualify for Hong Kong’s fund tax concessions based on the individual portfolio’s profits, even if the wider fund lags. Eligibility could stretch to analysts, quant researchers and fundraising staff, without a direct interest in fund profits; fixed or guaranteed sign-on bonuses, management-fee-linked pay and carry accrued before 1 April 2025 are excluded, and records must be kept for seven years. Point72 and QRT are expanding there. Worth watching for IR and BD people weighing Hong Kong against Singapore, Dubai and Abu Dhabi.
