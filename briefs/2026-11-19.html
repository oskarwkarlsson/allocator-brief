Maryland’s $81bn pension is taking its $4bn hedge fund book to zero

The Maryland State Retirement and Pension System has started winding down its Absolute Return portfolio (5.5% of assets at June 2025) and expects to be out of hedge funds by October 2028, moving the money into private credit and infrastructure. Last year the book made 10.7% against 16.3% for its stocks; managers have included Brevan Howard, ExodusPoint, King Street, Kirkoswald and Bridgewater’s Pure Alpha. New Mexico PERA is weighing a similar wind-down. Expect consultants at other US public plans to ask the same question, while ADIA and CPP are still adding.

QIA’s CEO calls the AI sell-off a “blip” and keeps the $500bn US plan

Mohammed Al-Sowaidi told Semafor that “a quarter of a blip or a year of correction doesn’t really mean much for us as a long-term investor”, said the $580bn fund’s US commitment is unchanged despite the war hitting Qatar’s LNG, and confirmed QIA put in for more Anthropic shares in the IPO. Hours later Anthropic priced at $83, raising $81.3bn, the largest IPO on record. A useful line for anyone pitching AI-linked strategies to Gulf sovereigns this quarter.

Aschenbrenner plots “Situational Awareness 2.0”, four months after a 67% loss

The FT reports the 25-year-old has discussed returning to leveraged trading and raising outside capital, possibly in 2027, though nothing is final. The fund has grown to about 30 staff from eight in June, is hiring a chief risk officer, and is talking to Jefferies about prime brokerage after JPMorgan stopped lending in July. It still runs over $10bn, mostly private, including an Anthropic stake of about $5bn. Any LP asked to back a re-levered relaunch will want to see risk governance first.

Hedge funds lost 1.2% in October, yet still took in $4.1bn

Citco’s monthly update shows a second losing month (macro -4.5%, equity -2.4%, multistrategy -0.1%), with $1bn to $3bn managers hit hardest at -2.9%. Net inflows still ran for a tenth month, $136bn year to date, and multistrats took $3.6bn of October’s $4.1bn. Money keeps concentrating at the big platforms even in a bad month, which is the backdrop for every mid-sized raise.
