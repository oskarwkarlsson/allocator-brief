Hedge funds sit 4% down for November as Nvidia’s 31% rebound burns the shorts, Mubadala’s US book shrinks 41% and Riyadh orders Saudi staff into half of finance jobs

Top stories
Perf

A 4% November drawdown, then the biggest one-day stock gain on record

Global hedge funds lost more than 3% in the week to 20 Nov, their worst week since April 2025, taking November to about -4%, per a JPMorgan client note reported by Reuters; Goldman says equity long/short net exposure is back near its lowest since before Covid. Then on Monday Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang and closed up 31.5%, adding about $950bn of market value and costing short sellers about $11.2bn on the day, per ORTEX via MarketWatch. ORTEX says only about 1% of the float was short, so this reads as a news reaction rather than a squeeze. Expect investors to ask two things this week: what you cut at the lows, and whether you were short into Monday.

Gulf

Mubadala's US-listed portfolio falls 41% to about $21bn

Mubadala's 13F shows 72 holdings worth nearly $21bn at 30 Sep, down from just under $35bn at 30 Jun, almost entirely because GlobalFoundries fell 42% in the quarter; that one stake is 93% of the reported book. It exited 31 names, added 32 (including SpaceX, Adobe, Dell and Pfizer) and trimmed 16 including Nvidia and Palantir; a bitcoin ETF is its second-largest holding at $697m. The 13F is a narrow slice of a $385bn fund, but it is the number Abu Dhabi counterparties will see quoted, so pitch around the broader portfolio, not the filing.

Rules

The FCA will tell UK pension trustees not to fire managers on returns alone

After complaints from managers who say trustees dismissed them for avoiding concentrated US tech, the FCA is preparing to remind trustees to judge managers against their stated objectives, risk, style, service and costs, not just short-term performance, per an FT report summarised by Hedgeweek. The FCA and The Pensions Regulator are also consulting on a value framework due in 2027. With the Nasdaq 100 in a bear market last week, diversifying and low-net managers now have a regulator-backed line for UK review meetings.

Gulf

Saudi Arabia wants 50% Saudi staff in 17 finance roles by 23 May

Announced on Sunday 22 Nov by the Ministry of Human Resources, the rule covers private firms with two or more staff in roles including investment manager, investment adviser, financial analyst and financial brokerage manager, with a six-month grace period. It also bars outsourcing Saudisation-eligible jobs outside the kingdom. Any manager that opened a Riyadh office to be close to PIF and Hassana should check its headcount mix now, since IR and BD hires there are directly in scope.
