Gulf

The Economist asks what Saudi Arabia's PIF is actually for

With about $910bn under management, the PIF generated an investment loss of 2.2% in 2024 and twice that in 2025, with nearly $30bn of cumulative impairments over the two years while global stocks rose more than 40%, according to calculations by Tim Callen of the Arab Gulf States Institute cited by The Economist (22 Nov). The fund formalised a more domestic strategy in August yet put roughly a tenth of its assets into three US companies (Electronic Arts, SpaceX and Paramount). For managers pitching Riyadh, the read is a fund under pressure to show profit and keep capital at home, so expect harder questions on why an external mandate beats a local project. The Houthi strike on PIF's tower was covered on 20 Nov.

Asia's regulators spend the weekend propping up markets

Taiwan's FSC banned short sales below the prior close from 23 Nov, raised short-sale margins to 150% and paused new put-warrant listings, with hedging exemptions for securities and futures firms. Korea readied its 10.76tn won stock stabilisation fund for the first time in 18 years but ruled out Taiwan-style curbs, promising a crackdown on illegal shorting instead. The Kospi still fell as much as 4.4% on Monday morning. Asia long/short and quant managers now have a live short-constraint story to explain to investors this week.

Hedge fund seats are now judged against private credit, not cash

HedgeCo's Sunday analysis ties the week together: Maryland's $80.6bn system exits its roughly $4bn absolute return book by October 2028 for private credit and infrastructure, New Mexico PERA's CIO wants hedge funds wound down to zero, and HPS's new $9.5bn loan fund targets about 10%. Yet Citco funds took $4.1bn in October, a tenth straight month of inflows, with multi-strategy drawing $71.3bn of $136bn year to date, funds above $10bn taking $3.9bn and the $1bn to $3bn cohort losing $1.3bn. For mid-sized managers, the pitch now has to beat a contractual 10%, not T-bills plus 4.

Gulf

Trump's data-centre threat lands on ADIA, GIC and CPP deals

Global SWF warns that Trump's 20 Nov threat to penalise US companies that build data centres overseas could complicate billions of dollars of sovereign and pension investment, naming GIC, CPP Investments and ADIA among investors funding that international build-out. The White House gave no rate, mechanism or legal basis, per CNBC. Gulf allocators have leaned hard into AI infrastructure, so any manager selling digital-infrastructure exposure into Abu Dhabi should be ready for the policy-risk question. Global SWF's full piece is paywalled.
