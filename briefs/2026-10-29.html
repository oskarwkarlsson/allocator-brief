Gulf

Britain will tax sovereign funds on UK property income and gains from January 2029

The Budget ends the informal sovereign immunity for directly held UK real estate and some trading income, softened by a bespoke REIT route at 15%, an exemption for UK investment offices and a new status for state pension funds. The Telegraph puts the take at about £700m and says Gulf funds such as QIA are hit hardest. Expect Gulf allocators to revisit UK property structures; the consultation runs to January 20.

Mubadala Capital's head of ventures, Ibrahim Ajami, will leave to launch Noha, with Mubadala seeding a "few hundred million" of a $500m target

About 90% would go to early-stage US AI startups, pitched on access to UAE capital, customers and compute. A textbook anchored spinout: the sovereign keeps the relationship and the deal flow while the manager goes independent. Single source (FT, people familiar; talks ongoing).

Investcorp raised "north of $7bn" in its year to June and hit $64bn of assets, beating targets despite the war

The Mubadala-backed Bahraini firm says more than three quarters of its fundraising now comes from outside the Gulf and it aims for another $7bn-plus this year on the way to $100bn. A useful data point that Gulf-rooted managers are raising globally, not just at home.
